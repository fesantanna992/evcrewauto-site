03

The battery, honestly.

The battery is usually the most valuable part of an EV, and most of its condition lives in the vehicle's data, not in what you can see on a test drive. Here is what that data can — and can't — tell you.

What we can show you

  • How evenly the cells are balanced (the spread between the highest and lowest cell)
  • Insulation resistance against the safety-standard minimum
  • Temperatures, charge level and battery-related fault codes
  • Lifetime energy the battery has taken in, when the vehicle reports it
  • State of Health, when the manufacturer exposes it

What no mobile inspection can do

  • Predict exactly how long a battery will last
  • Replace a laboratory capacity test
  • Run a manufacturer's full battery test that takes many hours of controlled charging
  • Read the same data on every car — it varies by brand, model year and tool
  • Guarantee future reliability or repair costs

07

Questions people ask.

Why does an EV need a different inspection?

An EV's most valuable systems — the high-voltage battery, its management system, charging hardware, thermal management and electronics — mostly show their condition through the vehicle's data. Reading and interpreting that data takes EV-specific tools and experience.

Can you tell me exactly how long the battery will last?

No one can. Battery life depends on use, charging habits and climate. We report the battery data available on the day of the inspection and explain what it means.

What is battery State of Health (SoH)?

An estimate of the battery's current capacity compared with new. Manufacturers calculate and expose it differently, and some don't report it at all, so it's one useful indicator, not the whole picture.

Can you run Tesla's full Battery Health Test?

Not during a mobile inspection — it can take many hours and specific charging conditions. Our battery assessment reads what the vehicle reports. Ask us if you need a more extended evaluation.

Do you inspect hybrids, plug-in hybrids and gas cars?

Yes — all of them, with the same packages and prices. Electric and hybrid vehicles are our specialty; on a gas car, the high-voltage items don't apply and we focus on everything else on the checklist.

Can you inspect at a dealership or a private seller?

Yes, with the seller's permission. Tell us where the car is when you request the inspection and we'll coordinate access.

Who drives on the road test?

The owner or seller drives; we ride along and observe. The road test is part of the Pro package and needs the owner's consent.

Does the car need to be charged?

For an EV: Basic needs at least 20% charge; Pro needs 20% to 50%, with enough range to reach the DC fast charger we'll use. For a gas car, just enough fuel for the road test.

Do you repair cars?

Not yet. Today we diagnose and explain what we find. Mobile maintenance and repair are next on our list.

Does an inspection guarantee the car won't have problems?

No. An inspection shows the condition and information available at the time. It can't find every hidden, intermittent or future problem, and it doesn't guarantee battery life or repair costs. The purchase decision is always yours.
